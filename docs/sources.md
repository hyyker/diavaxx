# Clinical reference notes

Reviewed 8 September 2026. These data power a record checklist, not a clinical decision system. A displayed completion means a count/history criterion is met; it does not certify protection, schedule validity or eligibility.

## Finnish programme

[THL: national programme by age](https://thl.fi/nakemyksemme/korkeasta-rokotuskattavuudesta-on-pidettava-huolta/kansallinen-rokotusohjelma-mita-rokotteita-eri-ikaisille-suositellaan-) is the country reference, replacing Rokotesuoja.

The source lists rotavirus at 2, 3 and 5 months; DTaP-IPV-Hib and pneumococcal doses at 3, 5 and 12 months; first MMR at 12 months; varicella at 18 months; a DTaP-IPV booster at 4 years; and second MMR/varicella at 6 years. HPV is offered at 10–12 and dTap at 14–15. Childhood influenza eligibility runs from six months through six years.

Adults should have protection against the MMR diseases from vaccination or past disease; varicella vaccination is relevant without existing protection. dTap is scheduled at 25 and during pregnancy; dT at 45 and 65, then every ten years. Older-adult flu and indicated COVID vaccination need recurring review. THL's overview does not provide a single general older-adult COVID cutoff, so the 65+ checklist exposes it for eligibility review rather than prescribing a dose.

BCG, TBE, hepatitis A/B, additional pneumococcal and meningococcal vaccination depend on risk or circumstances. They remain available through personal tracking, but are not automatically included for everyone.

## German programme

[BMG: protective vaccinations](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/themen/praevention/impfungen/schutzimpfungen) provides the general programme: childhood DTP, polio, Hib, hepatitis B, pneumococcal, meningococcal B, MMR, varicella and rotavirus, followed by adolescent HPV and MenACWY. Early childhood pneumococcal/MenB references contain three doses. HPV is offered at 9–14 with catch-up through 17; later starts can require three doses.

Adult dT boosters recur every ten years; pertussis is combined with the next suitable booster. Adult measles catch-up depends on birth after 1970 and documented history. The app's measles target represents the full recorded course, not a direction to administer two new adult doses. Flu, pneumococcal and shingles vaccination enter the general 60+ checklist. Routine RSV vaccination enters at 75; younger risk groups need individual review. Infant RSV prevention uses antibodies and is not a vaccination entry.

The newer [RKI July 2026 COVID update](https://edoc.rki.de/handle/176904/13784.2) takes precedence over the BMG page's older baseline-immunity description. It withdraws the general three-antigen-contact requirement. The app includes routine seasonal COVID vaccination at 75+, while lower-age risk indications remain personal decisions. COVID illness logs do not use the withdrawn rule.

## Age groups and limits

The selector uses under 1, 1–5, 6–8, 9–14, 15–17, 18–59, 60–64, 65–74 and 75+. Broad groups include course/history review and doses scheduled later in the stage; inclusion does not mean a dose is due today. For example, Finnish HPV starts at 10 despite the 9–14 selector, flu stops at 6 despite the 6–8 selector, and the infant checklist includes the upcoming MMR/varicella course. The chooser explains these boundaries.

The app records primary-course reference targets and manually entered review dates. It does not calculate precise due dates, childhood booster totals, product-specific minimum intervals, pregnancy schedules or catch-up regimens. DTP and polio targets of three represent primary-series references, with boosters explicitly separate. Old doses remain in the history when a later age group excludes their disease. Select personal Track to keep it in the active checklist.

## Reference counts without a country plan

[WHO routine-immunization tables](https://www.who.int/teams/immunization-vaccines-and-biologicals/policies/who-recommendations-for-routine-immunization---summary-tables), including the [December 2025 table](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/immunization_schedules/immunization-summary-table-1.pdf), inform the common course references. Product and age alternatives appear next to each target in the app.

Examples include three-dose DTP/IPV references, two-dose MMR/varicella references (standalone rubella uses WHO's one-dose reference), BCG and injectable typhoid at one dose, two-dose pre-exposure rabies, three-dose TBE, and four-dose childhood malaria vaccination in endemic settings. These examples must not be applied as universal individual schedules. Post-exposure rabies care requires urgent clinical assessment.

## Illness histories

THL accepts prior MMR diseases and varicella history as relevant protection evidence. diavaxx requires the user to record both clinician/laboratory confirmation and recovery before applying that criterion automatically. This is a conservative product rule, not verification of the diagnosis. Unconfirmed supported histories need review; any illness without recorded recovery blocks automatic completion while the disease is actively tracked.

Other diseases retain their illness histories without automatically replacing vaccination counts. This avoids unsupported assumptions about natural immunity, including tetanus, shingles and infections whose duration of protection or vaccine needs vary. An explicit personal review status or due reminder still takes precedence over completion.
